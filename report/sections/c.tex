\subsection{TF-IDF classifiers}

The three TF-IDF classifiers take the sparse n-gram vectors described
in part (b). Logistic regression maps a weighted sum of the features
to a positive-class probability. The linear SVM produces a margin score,
which is converted to a probability by sigmoid calibration on grouped
folds within the training split. Complement Naive Bayes estimates
each class's feature weights from reviews in the other class, with
additive smoothing.

The feature and classifier settings were selected by grouped five-fold
cross-validation within the training portion. Each selected model was
then fitted on 5,909 reviews and evaluated on the shared validation
set of 1,492 reviews. For test prediction, both the TF-IDF vectoriser
and the classifier were refitted on all 7,401 labelled reviews.

\subsection{GloVe embeddings and BiLSTM}

The mean-embedding classifier averages the GloVe vectors available for
the tokens in a review. If none of the tokens has a pre-trained vector,
the review is represented by a zero vector. The resulting 300 features
feed a regularised logistic regression classifier.

The BiLSTM uses the same training vocabulary to initialise an
embedding matrix of size $V \times 300$, where $V$ is the vocabulary
size. Entries without pre-trained vectors are initialised from a
zero-mean Gaussian with standard deviation 0.05, and the padding row
is fixed at zero. The input is a batch of integer token IDs of shape
$B \times L$, where $B$ is the batch size and $L \leq 256$.

In each layer, the backward direction reverses only the valid part of
the sequence. Both pooling operations ignore padding. Max pooling
keeps the strongest activation in each channel, while mean pooling
summarises the valid states. The two pooled vectors are concatenated
to give the classifier $4H$ features, or 512 when $H=128$.

The BiLSTM was trained with Adam and cross-entropy. In the weighted
variant, class $j$ receives a loss weight of $n/(2n_j)$, where $n$ is
the number of training reviews and $n_j$ is the number in that class.
The best epoch was selected by validation Macro-F1 at a threshold of
0.5. Softmax gives the positive-class probability, and the validation
and test probabilities were averaged across seeds 42, 43, and 44.

\subsection{RoBERTa-base}

\paragraph{Input and classifier.}
After the light cleaning described in part (b), each review retains its
original casing. The RoBERTa tokenizer splits the text into subwords
and limits the sequence to 256 tokens, including the special tokens.
Longer sequences are truncated at the end. Within each batch, shorter
sequences are padded to match the longest one, and an attention mask
distinguishes the input tokens from padding.

The encoder produces a 768-dimensional representation at each token
position. The classification head reads the representation at the
first token, \texttt{\textless s\textgreater}. A dense layer with
\texttt{tanh} activation transforms this vector, with dropout before
and after the layer during training. The final linear layer gives
one score per class. Softmax converts the two scores to probabilities,
and the positive-class probability is retained for prediction.

\paragraph{Fine-tuning.}
The encoder and classification head were fine-tuned together on the
5,909 training reviews from the shared split. Each of the three final
runs used one epoch, FP32 precision, a batch size of 4, and unweighted
cross-entropy. AdamW used a weight decay of 0.01. The learning rate
increased to \(2 \times 10^{-5}\) over the first 10\% of training
steps and then decayed linearly to zero. Part (d) describes how
we selected this schedule.

\paragraph{Prediction.}
The data split and training settings were fixed across the three
runs, with random seeds 42, 43, and 44. For each review, the three
positive-class probabilities were averaged before applying the
decision threshold. Part (e) explains how the threshold was chosen
on the validation set and reports the resulting test predictions.
